# MoneyQuest — Multiplayer Financial Life Simulation
## Game Concept & Educational Platform Proposal

**Working title:** MoneyQuest  
**Audience:** Elementary through Junior High / approximately ages 8–14  
**Format:** Multiplayer digital game / classroom platform  
**Potential partner:** Bank / financial institution  
**Core idea:** Players start with nothing and build a life through work, money management, relationships, business, credit, saving, and unexpected events.

---

# 1. The Core Idea

MoneyQuest should NOT feel like a financial-literacy worksheet turned into a game.

The player starts with:

- $0 cash
- $0 savings
- No job
- No credit
- No possessions
- No skills
- No house
- No business

The player has to build a life.

They must:

1. Find a job.
2. Apply for work.
3. Choose part-time or full-time employment.
4. Earn money.
5. Pay for necessities.
6. Find housing.
7. Buy food.
8. Pay utilities.
9. Manage health and dental needs.
10. Decide when to save.
11. Build an emergency fund.
12. Establish credit.
13. Use or avoid debt.
14. Purchase transportation.
15. Develop skills.
16. Advance to better-paying jobs.
17. Start or join businesses.
18. Manage personal and business finances.
19. Deal with unexpected events.
20. Pursue personal goals.

The central game question is:

> **What kind of life do you want to build—and can you afford it?**

---

# 2. The Most Important Design Principle

## Never teach a financial concept until the player has a reason to care about it.

Don't tell a child:

> "Today we're learning about compound interest."

Instead:

> "You need $500 by Day 20. How are you going to get there?"

The child discovers the financial concept by making decisions and experiencing consequences.

The game should teach:

**CHOICES → CONSEQUENCES**

rather than:

**LECTURE → QUIZ**

# 4. State Selection

At the beginning of a game/classroom simulation, the teacher or game administrator selects a U.S. state.

The game then uses that state's current minimum wage as the baseline for entry-level employment.

The U.S. Department of Labor maintains the authoritative state minimum-wage table and updates it over time. The game should retrieve or update its wage database rather than hard-code rates permanently.

Example current rates from the Department of Labor's July 1, 2026 table include:

- California: $16.90/hour
- Washington: $17.13/hour
- Arizona: $15.15/hour
- Texas: $7.25/hour
- New York: $16.00–$17.00/hour depending on location
- Colorado: $15.16/hour

Some states have regional, employer-size, tipped-worker, youth, or other special rules, so the game should have a configurable wage engine rather than a single number per state.

Source: U.S. Department of Labor, State Minimum Wage Laws, updated July 1, 2026.

# 53. Important Technical Design Considerations

## Wage Data

The game should use a configurable wage database.

The U.S. Department of Labor's current state minimum-wage table should be treated as an authoritative source for baseline state rates.

Rates need periodic updates because many states use annual adjustments.

Some states also have regional, employer-size, tipped-worker, youth, or other special provisions.

## Financial Calculations

The game should have a configurable rules engine for:

- wages
- overtime
- taxes
- interest
- credit
- loans
- rent
- insurance
- utilities
- inflation
- business expenses

## Age Appropriate

The game should progressively reveal complexity.

Children should not need to understand APR or amortization to play the early game.

---

# 54. The Fundamental Design Philosophy

MoneyQuest should never tell the player:

> "This is the right financial decision."

Instead it should show:

> **"Here are your choices."**

Then:

> **"Here is what happened."**

Then allow the player to try again.

The most valuable learning can come from making a bad decision in a safe simulation.

## Initial Product Vision

> **MoneyQuest is a multiplayer life simulation that starts every player with nothing. Players must find work, earn income, manage housing, food, utilities, healthcare, transportation and entertainment while learning to save, use credit, borrow responsibly, build businesses and deal with unexpected events.**
>
> **Instead of teaching financial concepts through lectures and quizzes, MoneyQuest lets children experience financial decisions in a safe, social environment where choices have consequences.**
>
> **The goal isn't to create the richest player. The goal is to understand how decisions, trade-offs, risk and planning shape a person's financial life.**
